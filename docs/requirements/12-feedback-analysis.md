# 12. Analysis of Player Feedback

What players wrote to the Site Owner between 2021 and October 2026, and what it shows about them.

## 12.1 What was read, and its limits

The Site Owner's mailbox was searched on 13 October 2026, with permission, for mail about the site. Of 200 threads found, about 105 were from players. The rest were analytics reports, advertising and acquisition approaches, and solicitations, which are not analysed here.

- Only the subject and the opening lines of each thread's first message were read, except for the two feedback messages about the redesign, which were read in full.
- Threads were sorted into themes by keyword, so the counts are approximate. A thread can fall under more than one theme.
- People who write in are not a fair sample of players. They are the ones who hit a problem and cared enough to report it, in English.
- Nothing here identifies a sender. No names, addresses, or quotations are kept in this repository.

Player threads per year: 9 in 2021, 16 in 2022, 14 in 2023, 21 in 2024, 17 in 2025, and 28 so far in 2026.

## 12.2 Themes

| Theme                        | Threads  | Typical message                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Missing or late data         | About 50 | A new pokemon, forme, or mega evolution is not there yet; a pokemon cannot be given a move it learns; asks when an update is due. |
| Praise                       | About 25 | The site is the writer's favourite or the best they have used, usually followed by a report.                                      |
| Wrong analysis result        | About 15 | An ability, a move, or a type is scored wrongly, with the pokemon and the move named.                                             |
| Sprites or icons not showing | About 12 | A regional forme, a mega evolution, or a recent pokemon has no picture.                                                           |
| Saving teams                 | About 5  | The team is gone after a refresh or after the phone's browser reopens; asks for a way to save, or for an account.                 |
| Crash or blank page          | About 3  | Choosing one particular pokemon, or opening one filter, blanks the page.                                                          |
| Import, export, or sharing   | About 3  | Import did not work; asks for shareable links.                                                                                    |

Smaller themes, one to three threads each:

- Requests to support games outside the main series: a fan-made game built on one generation with later pokemon added, a fan game's egg moves, and a game played through a streaming extension.
- A request for a mode with no restrictions, so that any pokemon from any generation can share a team.
- Checklist: what "volt-turn" means; a ladder player asking for priority moves to be a checklist item.
- Showing the type of each move in the move list.
- Advertisements getting into screenshots of a team on a tablet.
- Permission to show the site in a video.
- Pokemon whose two genders look different having only one of them.

## 12.3 What the feedback shows

**Current and complete data is what players care about most.** Nearly half of all player threads are about something missing. The messages cluster after each release: the Hisuian pokemon in 2022, Generation 9 in late 2022, and the Legends: Z-A mega evolutions in the first half of 2026, when at least eight players asked when they would be added. This backs the Site Owner's 2-day target for major data (BR-3).

**Players do report wrong results, and they are specific.** About fifteen threads name a pokemon, a move or ability, and what the score should be. The 7-day target for fixing them (BR-7) relies on this.

**Missing pictures are the third most common complaint, after missing data and wrong results.** A pokemon without a sprite or an icon reads to players as a broken site, not as a small gap. So every pokemon and item needs a picture, even when Pokemon Showdown's images cannot be reached (issue 76).

**Saving teams was asked for from 2021 on.** Players asked for a way to keep a team, and one for an account. The redesign's saved teams answer the first without the second.

**Many writers are not competitive players.** They build teams for a playthrough, for a fan-made game, for characters of their own, or for fun, and several write from school addresses. One competitive ladder player identified themselves as such; no message was about tiers, doubles formats, or tournament preparation. This matches the Site Owner's aim of serving the non-competitive community (BR-9).

**The redesign's first day gave the clearest single finding.** Within hours of the redesign first going live on 10 October 2026, two players wrote that choosing one generation or game stopped them from building the team they wanted: one mixes pokemon from every generation, and one plays a fan-made game that combines two generations. The Site Owner put the old website back the same day and made Generations 6 to 9 combined the default before the redesign went live again on 13 October. A restriction that is right for a competitive player is a barrier for a non-competitive one, and the default has to serve the second.

Both of those messages came without a reply address, so they could not be answered. The form already says an email is only needed for a reply, and leaving it out is how a player stays anonymous.

## 12.4 Effect on the personas

Maya (P-1), the in-game player on a phone, is well supported: playthrough teams, phone and tablet use, lost teams, and unexplained terms all appear. Leo (P-5), who builds dream teams and teams for fan games, was added from this analysis, from the first-day feedback and the requests for fan games and unrestricted teams. Both are in [02-stakeholders-and-personas.md](02-stakeholders-and-personas.md).

Three personas drafted before this analysis were dropped. A competitive singles player had weak support: one ladder player, a few precise reports of wrong results, and two or three messages about import. A doubles player who writes in Spanish had weak support: writers from Brazil and France appear, but none mentions doubles, backups, or another language. A returning player of the older games had none: nobody wrote about Generations 1 to 5 or about old links. Silence in a mailbox is weak evidence, since a player whose import works does not write in, so such players may well use the site; it is just not built around them.
